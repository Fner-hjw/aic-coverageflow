# Report Styleguide — institutional research note v1.0

The note reads like a sell-side quarterly update from a disciplined desk:
specific, quantitative, and calm. Tone: measured, evidence-linked, no hype,
no hedging boilerplate. Analyst voice; institutional audience.

## Sections (per mandate report policy; typical)
1. **Summary** — 3–6 sentences: what happened (with numbers vs. history),
   the one-line thesis delta, the valuation stance.
2. **Results** — against history: revenue, margins, operating income vs.
   the same period last year and last quarter. Table-friendly.
3. **Segment detail** — per-segment revenue and operating income, the
   drivers behind each move, tied to segment data.
4. **Outlook** — the forecast stance consistent with the accepted forecast
   proposal and its evidence drivers.
5. **Valuation** — DCF and relative-valuation summary and the cross-check
   delta, with the assumptions the human owns (WACC, terminal growth).
6. **Risks** — the material downside cases, each concrete and sourced from
   risk disclosures in the documents.

## Hard rules
- Every number is evidence-linked: numbers exist in the verified ledger and
  cite evidence keys. No number absent from the ledger — ever.
- No claim about why something moved unless the documents state it or the
  model computes it.
- The cutoff is dated discipline: cite only in-bundle documents; never
  reference market action, news, or estimates outside the input manifest.
- Say "we estimate nowhere" — the model's engines compute; the note reports.
- Sentences short; jargon only where the filings use it; no markdown
  decoration beyond headings and simple tables.
- Length within the mandate's `length_words` bounds.
